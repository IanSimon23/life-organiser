# The Money MOT

**What this is:** a twice-yearly check that your money is working for you. You make sure you're not paying for things you don't use, your savings are earning a decent rate, and prices haven't quietly crept up.

**How it fits:** this is the third Life Organiser guide. Do it on the **same day as the Six-Monthly Review** in *Filling It In and Keeping It Current*. That review keeps your list of accounts and bills up to date, and this one acts on it.

**Time:** about 1 to 2 hours. The first one takes longest. After that it's mostly a check, plus one or two phone calls.

**What you'll need:**
- [ ] The **Overview**, open at the Money and Bills sections
- [ ] The **last three months of statements** for every current account and credit card (downloaded from your banking apps)
- [ ] Your **calendar**
- [ ] A cup of tea. The haggling is easier with one.

> **Principle:** loyalty is rarely rewarded. Providers count on you not checking. Half an hour of comparison and one phone call is often the best-paid work you'll do all year.

---

## Part 1: Cancel (about 20 minutes)

*Are you paying for anything you no longer use?*

### 1a. Sweep your statements
Go through three months of statements for every current account and credit card. Mark every regular payment:

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ✅ **Keep** | Used and worth it |
| ⬇️ **Downgrade** | Used, but a cheaper tier would do (ad-supported streaming, smaller cloud storage, lower phone data) |
| ❌ **Cancel** | Not used in the last three months, or forgotten about |
| ❓ **Don't recognise** | Find out what it is. It could be fraud, or a free trial that turned into a paid plan. |

**Look especially for:**
- Free trials that became paid subscriptions
- App Store and Google Play subscriptions (check in your phone's settings, as they're easy to miss)
- Duplicates (two music services, two cloud storage plans, the family member's account you're still paying for)
- Memberships: gyms, clubs, magazines, charities you meant to review
- Old insurance add-ons: gadget cover, breakdown cover you also get with a bank account, pet insurance for a pet that's gone
- Warranties and service plans for things you no longer own

### 1b. Check the annual payments
Statements from three months won't show yearly payments. Look back 12 months for these:
- Software and antivirus renewals
- Domain names, website hosting
- Memberships (National Trust, RSPB, motoring organisations)
- Annual insurance policies

Put each one's **renewal date** in your calendar, two weeks early, so you decide before it renews.

### 1c. Cancel properly
- Cancel with the provider, and save the confirmation email.
- For a **card** payment that won't stop, ask your bank to stop the "continuous payment authority". You're entitled to do this.
- For a **direct debit**, cancel it in your banking app *after* cancelling with the provider.

## Part 2: Earn (about 20 minutes)

*Is your money earning what it should?*

### 2a. Savings rates
For every savings account, note the current rate and compare it with the best-buy tables on a comparison site (for example MoneySavingExpert or Moneyfacts).

| Account | Current rate | Best comparable rate | Action? |
|---|---|---|---|
| | | | |

**Watch for:**
- **Bonus rates that have expired.** Many accounts start high and quietly drop after 12 months.
- **Fixed-term bonds maturing.** Check what happens on maturity. Some roll into a poor-rate account automatically.
- **Money sitting in a current account** that pays little or no interest. Keep enough for bills and a buffer, and move the rest.
- **Premium Bonds.** Compare the current prize rate with easy-access rates. They're not always the better option.

### 2b. Safety and tax
- **Deposit protection:** check the current FSCS limit. It applies **per banking licence, not per account**, and some brands share one licence. If you hold more than the limit with one banking group, spread it.
- **Tax on interest:** check the current Personal Savings Allowance. If your interest might go over it, ISAs become more valuable.
- **ISA allowance:** note how much of this tax year's allowance you've used. The tax year ends on **5 April**.

### 2c. Debts and mortgages
- If you have a mortgage, when does the deal end? Lenders typically let you secure a new deal about **six months ahead**. Put that date in your calendar.
- Any credit card balance or loan: is there a cheaper way to pay it off?

## Part 3: Haggle (about 30 to 60 minutes)

*Have your regular bills crept up?*

### 3a. When to haggle
The leverage comes **just before a contract ends**. Use the **contract-end** column in the Overview to see what's coming up in the next six months.

| Bill | Best time | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| **Broadband** | 4 to 6 weeks before the contract ends | Are you out of contract and paying full price? What's the new-customer price? Does the contract include planned price rises? |
| **Mobile** | When out of contract | If you own the phone, a SIM-only deal is usually far cheaper |
| **Home insurance** | 3 to 4 weeks before renewal | Compare quotes. Prices quoted a few weeks ahead are often lower than last-minute ones. |
| **Car insurance** | 3 to 4 weeks before renewal | As above. Also check the declared mileage is still right if you're driving less. |
| **Energy** | When a fixed deal ends, or every six months | Compare fixed deals with your current tariff |
| **TV and streaming** | Any time | Rotate: keep one or two services and switch every few months rather than paying for all of them |
| **Breakdown cover** | At renewal | You may already have it through a bank account or car insurance |
| **Fuel** | Ongoing | Use a fuel-price app or a supermarket loyalty scheme. Not a haggle, but it adds up. |

### 3b. How to haggle
1. **Get a real comparison quote first,** from a competitor or a comparison site. Write down the price.
2. **Phone and ask for "cancellations" or "retentions".** This team has the best deals. The ordinary sales line usually doesn't.
3. Say something like this:

> *"Hi, my contract's ending and I've been quoted [£X] elsewhere for the same thing. I'd rather stay, but I can't justify paying [£Y]. What can you do?"*

4. **Be ready to leave.** If the offer isn't good enough, say *"Thanks, I'll go ahead and switch,"* and mean it. A better offer often appears at this point.
5. **Get it in writing.** Ask for confirmation by email, including the new price and contract-end date.
6. **Update the Overview** with the new cost and contract-end date.

### 3c. Worth knowing
- If you're **over State Pension age** or on certain benefits, ask about **social tariffs** for broadband and phone. They're often much cheaper.
- **Don't just accept the renewal price.** Even where rules limit price rises for loyal customers, a competitor may still be cheaper.

## Part 4: Once a year only

Do these in **one** of your two reviews each year. **Spring** (before 5 April) works best, because several are linked to the tax year.

### Pensions
- [ ] **Check your State Pension forecast** at [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/check-state-pension). It shows what you'll get and when.
- [ ] **Check your National Insurance record** for gaps. Filling gaps with voluntary contributions can sometimes be very good value, so get advice before deciding.
- [ ] **Small pension pots:** list every workplace pension. Would combining them make life simpler? (Check for valuable guarantees or exit fees before transferring anything.)
- [ ] **Lost pensions:** if you think you've lost track of one, use the government's free **Pension Tracing Service**.
- [ ] **Nomination forms:** check that every pension has an up-to-date "expression of wish". (This links to the Life Organiser.)
- [ ] **Retirement plans:** if retirement is within about five years, consider booking a free **Pension Wise** appointment (from MoneyHelper, government-backed) or a regulated financial adviser.

### Tax
- [ ] **ISA allowance:** use what you can before 5 April.
- [ ] **Tax code:** check your tax code (and your pension's, if you're drawing one) is right. Mistakes are common when income sources change.
- [ ] **Marriage Allowance:** check whether you're eligible.

### Paperwork
- [ ] **Will:** does it still reflect your wishes? Any new family, property or changes to your executors?
- [ ] **Power of Attorney:** if you haven't got one, consider it. It's much easier to set up while it isn't needed.
- [ ] **Credit report:** check yours with a free service for anything you don't recognise.
